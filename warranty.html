A lifetime warranty that follows the car

Every Tint Man install comes with a free lifetime warranty. XPEL dealers honor it nationwide, and it transfers to the next owner when you sell.

What it covers

  • Bubbling
  • Peeling
  • Flaking
  • Color change
  • Delamination
  • Fading

It doesn't cover damage from abuse, such as seatbelt nicks or ammonia-based cleaners.

Keep the warranty card we hand you. It carries the roll number of your film, which lets any XPEL dealer confirm the film and handle a claim.

Caring for new tint

The first 30 daysHaze or small moisture bubbles are normal while the film dries. They clear on their own.
Rolling windows downKeep them up for two to three days.
CleaningWait at least five days. Skip ammonia cleaners and abrasive pads for the life of the film.
SeatbeltsGuide the buckle back by hand. A buckle that swings free can nick the film on the door.
Dotted glass edgesThe raised dots around some windows can look milky white, because the film sits on top of the dots and air settles between them.

A few windows can't take film: glass with certain factory coatings, and some sunroofs and windshields that already absorb a lot of heat. Window gaskets without a felt lining can scratch film. We check for all of this before we start.

How to inspect a finished job

We follow the visual inspection standard of the International Window Film Association, the trade group that sets the rules for the industry.

On your car

Look straight at the glass from three feet away, inside and outside, in natural daylight. Dirt specks, bubbles, creases and scratches should not stand out.

In your home or office

Look from six feet away, from inside the room, in daylight but out of direct sun. The standard leaves out a two-inch band around the edge of the glass.

While the film dries

Water haze and slight distortion during drying are not defects. Drying time depends on the film and the weather.

The gap at the top edge

A gap of 1/32 to 1/16 of an inch at the top of a car window is correct. It stops the gasket from lifting the film when the window moves.
